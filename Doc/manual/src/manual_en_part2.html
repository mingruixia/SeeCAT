DICOM to BIDS

Converts DICOM files into a BIDS dataset with dcm2niix. It reads the DICOM headers to find subjects, visits and series, names each series by rules, and writes the images with their JSON sidecars into the BIDS layout.

{{IMG dicom_page.png | DICOM to BIDS after Find subjects: the layout it recognised and one line per subject.}}

How subjects, sessions and series are found

  1. Reading headers. SeeCAT reads the first and last file of every folder that holds DICOM files (a fast reader of the header up to the series fields; other files, such as .txt or .nii, are ignored). A folder whose first and last files belong to different people is reported as an error.
  2. Subjects. Folders with the same PatientID belong to one subject. The subject is named after the folder that holds all of that person's folders and no one else's, keeping letters and digits (S_001 → sub-S001). When IDs are missing or anonymised (e.g. anonymous, xxxx, 0000), the folder tree decides. Layouts that work include one folder per subject (S001/…), modality folders with subject folders inside (T1/S001, REST/S001) and deeper trees (S001/visit1/T1). Find subjects shows the layout it recognised and the subjects before anything is converted.
  3. Sessions. A subject scanned in one visit (one DICOM study) gets no session folder. Several visits become ses-1, ses-2, … by scan date; the assignment is kept in code/seecat/from_dicom_sessions.tsv so a later visit gets the next number.
  4. Series. dcm2niix converts each subject; every series is named by the first rule that matches its sidecar. The default rules take a 3D T1-weighted image (MPRAGE, SPGR, BRAVO, …) as anat/…_T1w and resting-state BOLD (series names containing rest, rsfMRI, or RS as a word) as func/…_task-rest_bold. A resting run must also be a 2D EPI with at least 20 volumes. Series that match no rule are listed, not copied.
  5. Writing. Images go to sub-<label>/[ses-<label>/]anat|func as .nii.gz with their sidecars; dataset_description.json is written if missing and participants.tsv gets the new subjects. Images already present are kept, so converting again only adds what is missing. A table of every series and what happened to it is written to code/seecat/.

Data check

Before preprocessing, look at what the dataset holds. Data check lists every BOLD run of a BIDS dataset (or of a derivatives folder) with its number of volumes and TR; where a confounds table exists (after preprocessing or fMRIPrep), also the mean and largest framewise displacement and the frames flagged for motion. Runs with mean FD above 0.25 mm or more than 20 % of frames flagged are highlighted. Open in viewer steps through the volumes of the selected run with its motion trace; QC report opens its report.

Preprocessing

Preprocesses every BOLD run of a BIDS dataset with SPM and writes analysis-ready data to derivatives/seecat. Each run is a unit; its T1w (from the same session if there is one) is used for registration and segmentation.

{{IMG prep_page.png | The Preprocessing page of the example: input, preprocessing, VBM, denoising and quality-control cards.}}

Steps

  1. Input. The run and its T1w are copied into the work folder. Leading non-steady-state volumes are removed: with dummy_scans = auto, leading volumes whose mean brain intensity is a robust outlier (z > 3.5 against the median and MAD of the run, within the first 20) are dropped; volumes the scanner already discarded are not counted again.
  2. Slice timing (SPM). The slice acquisition times come from SliceTiming in the sidecar (seconds), so any order (sequential, interleaved, multiband) is handled; the reference is the middle of the TR unless set.
  3. Realignment (SPM). Two passes, to the first volume and then to the mean. The six motion parameters go to the confounds table; the data are not resampled here.
  4. Coregistration. The T1w is registered to the mean EPI by normalised mutual information (only its header changes).
  5. Segmentation. SPM unified segmentation of the T1w with SPM's tissue probability maps gives native GM, WM and CSF maps, a bias-corrected T1w and the deformation to IXI549Space. Without a T1w (normalise.method = epi) the mean EPI is segmented instead.
  6. Nuisance masks. WM (p > 0.99) and CSF (p > 0.95) masks in the subject's own space, eroded by a 2 mm ball, so they keep away from grey matter; they are resampled to the output grid.
  7. Normalisation. The deformation is applied once to the BOLD data (4th-degree B-spline; the only resampling of the data) and to the tissue maps, at the chosen voxel size over SPM's bounding box.
  8. Masks in output space: brain = GM + WM + CSF > 0.5, inside the field of view in every frame.
  9. Confounds. A table with fMRIPrep's column names: 24 motion regressors (6 parameters, their derivatives and squares; Friston 1996), framewise displacement (Power 2012), the mean signals of brain (global_signal), WM and CSF, aCompCor components of WM and CSF (computed on good frames), one motion_outlier_XX column per flagged frame and a censor column.
  10. IQMs (optional, on by default): MRIQC image quality metrics of the raw BOLD and T1w; see 6.5.
  11. Denoising. One linear model per voxel: polynomial trend (order 1) + the chosen regressors, with data and regressors band-passed by the same filter, fitted on the good frames. High-motion frames are first filled in from the good frames (spectral interpolation) so that filtering does not spread them, then left out of the fit and marked for later steps. The voxel mean is added back. Output: desc-denoised.
  12. Smoothing (optional): Gaussian kernel of the chosen FWHM; output desc-denoisedSm6. The unsmoothed series is kept.
  13. Outputs and a QC report per run; when all runs have finished, the group report and the exclusion list.
Why denoise before smoothing, and why filter regressors too. Regressing nuisance signals from smoothed data mixes signals of neighbouring tissue; band-passing the data but not the regressors reintroduces removed signal (Hallquist et al. 2013; Lindquist et al. 2019). SeeCAT filters both and fits them together. High-motion frames are interpolated before filtering because a spike would otherwise spread over its neighbours; the interpolated frames are never used by later statistics (Power et al. 2014).

fMRIPrep denoising

For data already preprocessed by fMRIPrep. For each BOLD run in the chosen space it reads *_desc-preproc_bold, its brain mask and confounds table, removes fMRIPrep's non-steady-state volumes, replaces fMRIPrep's motion outlier columns by SeeCAT's own (the same FD rule for every input), and then denoises, censors and smooths exactly as Preprocessing. Outputs have the same names, so Seed FC, Degree, Gradients and Statistics work unchanged. IQMs are not computed (fMRIPrep's outputs hold no raw data); the motion, coverage and registration checks are (registration uses fMRIPrep's tissue maps in the output space when present).

{{IMG fmriprep_page.png | The fMRIPrep denoising page.}} {{PARAMS fmriprep Input}}

aCompCor takes the first n c_comp_cor/w_comp_cor columns of fMRIPrep's table; older fMRIPrep versions write only a_comp_cor_*, which must then be listed by name in a config file (denoise.regressors).

Quality control

Quality control runs inside Preprocessing and fMRIPrep denoising; the QC reports page is where you review it and decide which runs are used.

The run report

Each run gets sub-*/…_desc-qc_report.html with:

  • a summary: frames, dummy scans, mean and largest FD, flagged frames, minutes kept, tSNR, the correlation of FD with DVARS before and after denoising (a good denoising brings it close to 0), largest translation and rotation, coverage;
  • head motion: FD over time with the threshold and flagged frames shaded, translations and rotations;
  • DVARS before and after denoising;
  • carpet plots (Power 2017) before and after denoising, voxels ordered GM, WM, CSF;
  • registration and coverage: the mean normalised EPI with the template GM outline and the brain mask, and the registration measures;
  • the WM and CSF masks used for the nuisance signals;
  • the image quality metrics (MRIQC).
{{IMG qc_run_report.png | Part of a run report: summary, head motion and DVARS.}}

Image quality metrics (IQMs)

With qc.iqm (on by default) Preprocessing computes the metrics of MRIQC (Esteban et al. 2017) on the raw data, reusing its own segmentation, realignment and coregistration: the BOLD run after dummy-scan removal, head-motion corrected, without slice timing; the T1w with its bias-corrected image and native tissue maps. The formula-based metrics give the same values as MRIQC's own functions on the same inputs; the head, air and brain masks are SeeCAT's own, so absolute values can differ from an MRIQC run. Coverage

The share of the template brain inside the field of view in every frame, in total and separately for its top (z > 50 mm) and bottom (z < −30 mm: cerebellum and temporal poles). Low coverage means part of the brain was not scanned.

Seed FC

Seed-based functional connectivity. For each run and seed, the mean signal of the seed's voxels (inside the brain mask, on the frames kept after censoring) is correlated with every voxel; the result is written as a Pearson r map and a Fisher z map (z = atanh r). Use the z maps in Harmonize and Statistics.

{{IMG fc_page.png | Seed FC with two spheres (PCC and mPFC). Spheres and masks can be mixed in the table; the Check column says what a row still needs.}}

Seeds

  • Sphere: a centre in MNI millimetres (x y z) and a radius; voxels within the radius of the centre. Examples: PCC 0 -53 26, mPFC 0 52 -6, left motor cortex -38 -22 56.
  • Mask: a NIfTI image in MNI space (any voxel size; voxels above 0 are the seed), resampled to the data by nearest neighbour. Add mask files adds one row per chosen file, named after it.

Names are letters and digits; they become the desc of the output maps.

Degree

Voxel-wise degree centrality. Every voxel of the mask is a node of the correlation network of a run (frames kept after censoring). The binary degree of a voxel counts its connections whose correlation reaches the threshold; the weighted degree (strength) sums their r values. As in SeeCAT 1.0: correlations from z-scored series, the sign rule applied (absolute: |r|; negative: −r), values below the threshold set to 0. Optionally connections shorter than a distance are ignored, or split into distance bins (short- and long-range degree). Maps are written raw and z-scored within the mask, and smoothed afterwards if asked (the network is built from unsmoothed data).

Time and memory grow with the square of the number of voxels: about 50 000 grey-matter voxels at 3 mm take seconds to a minute per run; at 2 mm there are 3.4 times as many voxels and over ten times the work.

Gradients

Connectivity gradients (Margulies et al. 2016): the main axes along which connectivity profiles change across the cortex, the first usually running from sensory and motor cortex to the default-mode network. For each run:

  1. the node series: the mean of the run's brain-mask voxels in each node (voxels of a coarser MNI grid, or parcels of an atlas), frames kept after censoring;
  2. the connectivity matrix: Pearson r between nodes;
  3. each row keeps its strongest 10 % (sparsity 0.9) and the rest is set to 0;
  4. the affinity between rows: normalised angle (1 − angle/π of their cosine similarity) or cosine;
  5. diffusion map embedding with α = 0.5 (Coifman & Lafon 2006) and multiscale eigenvalues λ/(1−λ), as BrainSpace (Vos de Wael et al. 2020);

then the runs are aligned (generalized Procrustes: rotations and sign flips onto a reference, the reference updated to the mean, up to 10 rounds), starting from the gradients of the group-mean connectivity matrix (default) or from the first run. SeeCAT's embedding equals the exact diffusion map; it solves the symmetric form of the problem.

Harmonize

Multi-site studies carry site and scanner differences in every map. Harmonize removes them from per-subject maps, between the measures (Seed FC, Degree, Gradients, VBM) and Statistics, while keeping the effects you will test. It writes the harmonised maps next to the originals with the desc label extended (desc-PCC → desc-PCCComBat).

{{IMG harmonize_page.png | Harmonize for the PCC maps of the example: site column, ComBat, and the covariates whose effects are kept.}}

Methods

MethodWhat it doesWhen
combatComBat with parametric empirical Bayes (Johnson et al. 2007; Fortin et al. 2017, 2018): per voxel, data = site location + covariate effects + error scaled per site; the site location and scale are estimated per voxel and shrunk across voxels, then removed.the default; small sites benefit from the shrinkage
combat_nonparamempirical Bayes without a distribution for the site effects (prior from up to 5000 other voxels)when the site effects are far from normal
combat_noeblocation and scale per voxel without shrinkagelarge sites
combat_meansite means onlywhen variances should stay as they are
combat_gamComBat with natural-spline terms for the Nonlinear covariates (Pomponio et al. 2020)wide age ranges, non-linear effects
covbatComBat, then the site differences in the covariance between voxels are removed (Chen et al. 2022)when network structure differs between sites
lmlinear regression: site means removed with the covariates in the modela simple reference

The ComBat variants give the same results as neuroCombat. A reference site can be kept unchanged with the others mapped onto it. Every effect you will test later must be among the kept covariates; otherwise, where it differs between sites, it is partly removed with the site effect. The log and group/harmonize<label>_<maps>/…_report.json give the share of voxels with a site effect (F test, p < 0.05, adjusted for the kept effects) before and after.

Small sites. The scale (variance) step of combat, combat_nonparam, combat_noeb, combat_gam and covbat divides each site's residuals by its own standard deviation. With few subjects per site those estimates are noisy and the harmonised data look less variable than they are, so later tests of the kept covariates become too liberal (Nygaard et al. 2016). In Example B (three sites of ten, simulated ages) a GM–age regression on ComBat-harmonised VBM maps found three clusters that are not there: none with combat_mean, none with lm, none with the unharmonised maps and site as a covariate. With fewer than about 20 subjects per site, SeeCAT writes a warning in the log and in the report. Then prefer combat_mean or lm, or leave the maps unharmonised and add the site column as a covariate in Statistics; compare with the unharmonised result before reporting.

Statistics

Group statistics on per-subject maps: a general linear model at every voxel, contrasts, and correction for multiple comparisons. The model, smoothness estimate and random field theory follow SPM, so the numbers match SPM's results table for the same model.

Corrections

  • cluster_fwe (default): clusters of voxels above the cluster-forming p (0.001), kept when their extent has a family-wise error p < alpha by random field theory.
  • voxel_fwe: voxels with FWE p < alpha (random field theory).
  • voxel_fdr: voxels with false discovery rate < alpha (Benjamini–Hochberg).
  • tfce_fwe: threshold-free cluster enhancement (Smith & Nichols 2009; E = 0.5, H = 2, 26-connectivity) with voxel FWE p from permutations (Freedman–Lane: sign flips for one-sample, swaps within subjects for paired designs, row permutations otherwise), as FSL randomise. Parallel workers share the permutations.

With Two-tailed (default), each T contrast of a pair is tested at alpha/2. Maps excluded by QC are left out, and every map that is dropped (no participants.tsv row, a missing value) is listed in the log.

Viewer

The viewer shows any NIfTI image on an MNI brain: SeeCAT's statistic maps, seed and degree maps, gradients, VBM maps, or a 4D BOLD series.

{{IMG viewer_ortho.png | The one-sample PCC result in the viewer: three views, the cursor read-out with the atlas label, and the cluster table.}}
  • Layouts: Orthogonal (three views and the cursor read-out), Single (one large view and three thumbnails), Mosaic (slices of one orientation).
  • Underlay: chosen by the map's space (ch2 for IXI549Space, ICBM152 2009c for MNI152NLin2009cAsym), or picked from the menu.
  • Threshold by value or by p. For SeeCAT statistic maps (and SPM-style maps with df in the header) p thresholds can be corrected (cluster FWE, voxel FWE, voxel FDR) with the same code as Statistics, so the viewer and the pipeline agree. Sign (+, −, ±), minimum cluster size, colour map and opacity.
  • Clusters: the table of clusters with their peaks and atlas labels (Neuromorphometrics from SPM; AAL and Brodmann when BrainNet Viewer is installed); clicking a row moves the cursor. Export TSV saves it.
  • Time series: for a 4D series, the cursor voxel's signal with FD and flagged frames; a slider and Play step through the volumes.
  • Save image writes the current view as PNG; Save map writes the thresholded map as NIfTI; Surface shows the map on the cortex in BrainNet Viewer.
  • Neuro / Radio: left on the left, or on the right.
